# Robot Rescue: setting and story

## The setting

**The Robot Rescue Bureau** is a cheerful little space station in orbit. Its job is keeping people safe wherever
everyday things go wrong. Its bots are small, brave and slightly wobbly. **You** are the newest: a rookie rescue bot you
build yourself in the Bureau workshop (body, head, feet, antenna, badge, colours, face and voice), and every friend who
joins builds their own. **PIP** is the name and look a rookie gets if you don't change anything, so this doc uses "PIP"
for whichever rookie you are. **DISPATCH** runs mission control and talks you through every call, by your bot's name,
a bit too fast and with too much coffee.

Every rookie is built from the same kit as **Unit Zero**, the Bureau's first rescue bot: the same sockets, the same face
language. That matters at the end.

## The theme

> Rescuers need rescuing, too. You don't have to do everything yourself to be useful.

Rookies start out treating every call as a machine to fix perfectly. Over the campaign they learn to notice what people
actually need: reassurance, patience, company, someone willing to ask for help. Unit Zero is the extreme version of the
same impulse: a bot that kept an important system running long after anyone should have expected it to stay on duty.
The finale fixes a machine and a character at once: the city learns to share a load one robot carried alone.

When a call comes in, the Bureau fires a **mission pod** down to wherever help is needed. Each destination is its own
seamless world: you land, then walk, climb and swing from call to call with no loading screens. Each call you finish
powers up the next bridge, gate or path. *Rescuing and building go together*: every rescue leaves the place a little
more connected than you found it.

## The mystery: the Hum

Little things keep going wrong, a bit more often than they should. The break room coffee machine over-pressurises.
Kitchen fuses blow. Belts run backwards. Every time, a low **hum** comes over the long-range radio. DISPATCH notices
the pattern (*short short short, long long long*): it's an **S.O.S.**, coming from under the sea at Brightwater Reach.

Years ago a storm tipped the **Old Rig**, and Unit Zero got everyone off. Then it stayed behind to keep the Reach's
anchor engine and tidal pumps turning. The rig half-sank and Zero has been minding the engine alone ever since. When
its battery runs low it hums, and the hum shakes the power grid. That's why the coffee machines keep blowing up.

**Why nobody came: a failed handover, not a cover-up.** During the evacuation a damaged controller reported that the
Old Rig had switched to a stable automatic mode. In fact its governor had failed and Zero stayed plugged in to do the
governor's job. Every reading the Bureau saw looked normal *because Zero was doing the work*. The records were wrong,
not the people. When the rookies prove it, the Bureau's answer is immediate concern, practical help and an honest
"we got this wrong", never denial. The home base stays a place you can trust.

**Not everything is the Hum.** Some broken things are just broken. The town feels real because its people have lives
beyond the mystery.

### How the Hum changes

| Stage | What players think | What the game shows |
|---|---|---|
| Early calls | "Something in this city rattles." | Small pulses in pipes, lamps and machines. |
| Middle chapters | "These keep following a pattern." | The same rhythm in the seismograph, old service records, unrelated districts. |
| Lighthouse | "Someone is transmitting." | Restored signal gear makes the S.O.S. unmistakable. |
| Old Rig approach | "Zero is still working down there." | The signal is a request for relief, not a threat. |
| Finale | "We need to take over safely." | Getting Zero out needs a working replacement and the whole community. |

Script note: DISPATCH currently decodes the S.O.S. in Chapter 2. A later script pass should let Chapter 2 only notice
a rhythm, Chapter 4 record it, and Chapter 5 (the lighthouse) confirm it as an S.O.S.

### Memory bolts

The golden bolts on masts and rooftops are **memory bolts**: keepsakes Zero clipped onto things over the years, not
fasteners holding anything up (so collecting them never weakens the city). Each plays a recording in the order you
find it (12 logs): an improvised recipe, a terrible joke, a repaired toy, a visit to the lighthouse. They make you love
Zero. They are never needed to understand the plot or to get the good ending; the essential story is on the main route.

Chapters 6 and 7.

## The ending is a handover, not a boss fight

Secure the installation, prepare the replacement, stabilise the system, test the transfer, release Zero, bring
everyone home. Each phase stays done once done. No sacrifice, no combat, no brand-new mechanic at the last second.

The people you helped show up for it: Nonna Lucia's kitchen feeds the crews, Priya's seismograph watches the engine,
Captain Mo's boat brings the cradle, the drone crews hold the second sockets. At the start the rookies help a city one
small problem at a time; at the end the city cares enough to help them bring the first rescuer home.

**Epilogue (playable):** the kitchen works, the lighthouse shines, the park is busy, and Zero is finally doing
something optional, sitting beside Mittens, while your bot takes another small call.

## How the mechanics fit the story

- **Battery and chargers**: everything in the Reach runs on one shared grid, Unit Zero's grid. A flat battery is a
  taste of what Zero has lived with for years.
- **Breakable parts**: Bureau bots are built to pop apart and click back together. Zero has been fixing itself with
  whatever drifts by.
- **Bridges and gates**: finishing a call powers the next bridge. Rescuing builds the city.
- **Customizable rookies**: everyone builds their own bot from Zero's kit. Different shells, same sockets, which is
  exactly why the rookies can take over Zero's job at the end.
- **Co-op (later)**: more bots means more hands. Teammates can share charge, carry each other's lost arms, and hold
  two sockets at once. Asking a friend for help is the theme, played.
